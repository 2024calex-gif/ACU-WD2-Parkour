## Findings

<!-- FINDINGS:BEGIN -->
Data limits first.

1. Only one patched SkipOver has bone data: session3 capture 191504_2 (Fd100cm-Up100cm). Every patched-vs-original bone comparison below therefore rests on n = 1 patched occurrence. session3 has pose_ok false in 13 of 15 captures (12 with no bone frames). Entity (root) data exists for all of them.
2. Frame-to-frame derivatives are dominated by timing noise. Root displacement barely tracks the frame dt (dp-vs-dt correlation between -0.6 and 0.6 in most captures; a constant-speed root would give close to 1), and hand speed jumps between about 1 and 14 m/s from one frame to the next. Raw jerk values are therefore only comparable inside a capture. Use the "x" ratio (window maximum over the capture's control median), not the raw m/s-per-frame value.

Where the staggers are.

3. In-transition (t_in): no excess. Patched 191504_2 peaks in the in-window at LeftHand x1.9, RightHand x3.8, Head x4.2, Hips x6.8, with the peaks at +0.07 to +0.12 s after t_in. The 11 original pose captures span LeftHand x1.1 to 4.3, RightHand x1.2 to 5.8, Head x2.3 to 14.1, Hips x1.6 to 24.6, and their peaks cluster at about +0.15 s. The patched values sit inside those ranges. The spike is in the middle of the bake fade-in (clip 0.45 to 0.50 s inside the 0.38 to 0.58 s fade). The original clip shows the same take-off peak with no fade present, so the fade does not create it. The cut at t_in has no pose step (boundary ratio 0.4 to 1.2 on every bone).
4. Out-transition (t_out): the one place the patch stands out. Patched 191504_2 peaks at LeftHand x3.4, RightHand x4.7, Head x6.3, Hips x7.8, all after t_out (+0.035 to +0.084 s). The original medians are LeftHand x2.1, RightHand x2.0, Head x3.4, Hips x4.1. The patched values are above the median on all four bones. RightHand (x4.7 against an original maximum of x4.4) and the Head rotation peak (962 deg/s against an original maximum of 730 deg/s) are above the original maximum. Only one patched capture supports this, so it is a lead, not a measured effect.
5. Out-transition timing: clip boundary, not the fade. The fade-out for the enabled clips runs from clip 0.89 to 1.14 s (Fd100cm-Up100cm), which is capture +0.51 to +0.76 s after t_in. The window end o + window = 1.138 s falls on the next clip start, because t_out - t_in is 0.750 s and window is 0.758 s (the three enabled clips agree to within about 0.04 s across the captures). The peak arrives at clip 1.165 to 1.214 s, 0.03 to 0.08 s after the bake has returned to the original pose (weight 0). So the stagger is at the game's cut to the next clip, not in the bake's fade-out. The pose step across that cut is small on the body bones (boundary ratio 0.4 to 0.9 on hands, head and hips; the feet reach 1.2 and 1.5), which means the next clip's first frames accelerate, not that the pose snaps.
6. Pose mismatch at the cut: the patched cut is the smooth one. Original cuts often snap. At t_out, 7 of 11 original pose captures have a step ratio of 2 or more on a hand or the head (up to 5.4, next clips LandingFwd and LandingOnSpot). Muraille cuts sit at 0.7 to 3.8 in the originals. Patched 191504_2 is at 0.4 to 0.9 on hands, head and hips (feet 1.2 and 1.5). The hypothesis that the patch's first or last frame mismatches the neighbouring clip is not supported here.
7. Entity root, all occurrences: a separate issue. Median root jerk ratio in the in-window: patched Fd010cm-Up100cm x10.2 (n=7) against original x4.9 (n=6); patched Fd100cm-Up100cm x1.7 (n=7) against original x1.4 (n=3). Out-window: x4.3 against x2.4 for Fd010cm, x2.9 against x1.5 for Fd100cm. The entity z plateau during the vault sits 0.43 to 0.48 m above the obstacle top in four patched Fd010cm captures (191527_4, 191533_5, 191549_8, 191602_10, all at z 1.04 to 1.05 m). Originals also overshoot in some cases (session1 captures 1 and 11 at +0.30 and +0.44 m), so the overshoot is not unique to the patch. The baked Reference translation for Fd010cm-Up100cm (2C62204A06) differs from the original dump by at most 3 cm at the sampled clip times (0.0 to 2.2 s), so the bake's translation track does not explain it. Cause open.

Fades and clip times.

8. Fade windows (T9e, bake2.fades_for, clock clip time = o + r (t - t_in)): Fd100cm-Up100cm, o = 0.380, window 0.758, fade-in clip 0.380 to 0.580 (capture 0.000 to 0.200 s after t_in), fade-out clip 0.888 to 1.138 (capture 0.508 to 0.758 s). Fd010cm-Up100cm, o = 0.591, window 0.786, fade-in 0.591 to 0.791 (capture 0.000 to 0.200), fade-out 1.127 to 1.377 (capture 0.536 to 0.786). Fd200cm-Up100cm (enabled, not in these captures): o = 0.380, window 0.470, fi = 0.141 and fo = 0.165. The bake's default FADE_IN 0.10 / FADE_OUT 0.15 apply only to the T9b path; T9e uses the per-clip values above.
9. Where the spikes fall in those windows: for the one pose-ok patched capture, the in-spikes sit in the middle of the fade-in and the out-spikes sit just after the fade-out ends. Neither lands on a fade boundary. The root-only patched Fd010cm captures have an out-window root spike at t_out - 0.15 to -0.19 s, which is inside the fade-out (clip 1.18 to 1.20 s). Original Fd010cm captures show the same root spike at the same time (-0.15 to -0.19 s), so it is clip motion that the fade overlaps, not a fade artefact.

Overall: the out-transition is where the patch differs most from the original. Its timing is the clip boundary (next clip start, just after the bake has returned to the original), so the fix to test is at the cut rather than the fade windows. The in-transition does not stand out. The one clear new effect in the patch is the entity root overshoot in the Fd010cm captures, which the baked translation does not explain.

Open, and what would settle it: (a) find out why the plugin writes pose_ok false (or no bone frames) in session3, so that patched occurrences get bone data; (b) sample the entity matrix and the frame time in the same call, so timing noise drops out of the derivatives; (c) a second patched SkipOver with pose_ok, and a pose capture of the unpatched Fd100cm-Up100cm for a same-clip control. Session2 was not analysed (not in the brief).
